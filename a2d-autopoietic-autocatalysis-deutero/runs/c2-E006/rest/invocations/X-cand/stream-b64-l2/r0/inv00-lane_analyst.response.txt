1. **R1–R2:** Deliver one Rust source file in a `rust` fence, compiling under edition 2024 with std only and no inner attributes/comments. Match every public signature and error field exactly; derive the required error traits and keep stream state private.

2. **R3–R4, R14:** Use the exact alphabet and MSB-first packing for all byte values. Emit zero unused tail bits, exactly `==` or `=` as required, and no newline. Avoid overflowing narrow intermediates when packing bytes.

3. **R3, R5, R9, R20:** Nothing is skipped: whitespace and non-ASCII bytes are invalid. References to “skipped” bytes or “framing state” introduce no skipping or framing syntax. Offsets count physical input bytes from zero.

4. **R6–R7, R16:** `=` at positions zero or one fails immediately. At position two it waits; the next byte must be `=`, otherwise report `BadPadding` at that next byte—even for whitespace or an invalid alphabet byte.

5. **R5, R8, R16:** Only `xxxx`, `xx==`, and `xxx=` complete valid groups. Once padding completes a group, every subsequent byte produces `BadPadding` at that byte; this takes precedence over invalid-byte classification.

6. **R10–R12, R16:** At EOF, pending position-two padding gives `BadPadding` at that `=`; otherwise an incomplete group gives `Truncated` at its first byte. Any earlier scan error wins; never precheck total length.

7. **R13:** Accept noncanonical unused bits: `Zh==` yields `f` and `Zm9=` yields `fo`. Encoder canonicality must not become a decoder validation rule.

8. **R4, R15, R17:** Empty one-shot input succeeds with empty output. Calls and newly created streams are independent; empty pushes on open streams succeed without flushing or changing buffered data.

9. **R18, R23:** Encoder pushes emit every available complete three-byte group and retain only zero to two bytes. Only finish emits a padded tail; finish closes even an empty stream.

10. **R19–R20, R24:** Decoder groups, pending padding, terminal padding, and absolute offsets survive every chunk boundary. Emit padded output immediately upon its fourth symbol; successful finish has no residual bytes to emit under these rules.

11. **R15, R21:** One-shot errors expose no partial output. A failing decoder push likewise exposes none of that call’s completed groups, while outputs from earlier successful pushes remain valid.

12. **R22–R24:** Every decoder error is sticky, preserving its exact variant and fields without further scanning. Successful finish makes subsequent operations return `AlreadyFinished`; completed padding alone leaves the decoder open, so an extra byte before finish produces `BadPadding`.
